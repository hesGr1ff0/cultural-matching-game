# Culture Match: content

## 1. Feathered war bonnet
- Culture: Plains nations (Lakota, Cheyenne, Crow)
- Category: Dress
- Fact: Among Plains nations, a warrior historically earned each eagle feather through a brave deed, so a war bonnet signalled its wearer's accomplishments.
- Source: Encyclopaedia Britannica. https://www.britannica.com/topic/war-bonnet

## 2. Maasai shuka
- Culture: Maasai (Kenya and Tanzania)
- Category: Dress
- Fact: The shuka is the predominantly red cloth of the Maasai, pastoralists of Kenya and Tanzania, and is one of the most recognisable symbols of Maasai identity.
- Source: National Museums of Kenya. https://artsandculture.google.com/story/maasai-crafts-and-fashions-national-museums-of-kenya/ggURaG-t_WgykA
- Source: Encyclopaedia Britannica. https://www.britannica.com/topic/Maasai

## 3. Kente cloth
- Culture: Asante and Ewe peoples (Ghana)
- Category: Textile
- Fact: Kente is made by stitching together narrow strips of handwoven cloth, and its weaves, patterns and colours often carry symbolic meaning.
- Source: Victoria and Albert Museum. https://collections.vam.ac.uk/item/O164606/kente-textile-unknown

## 4. Kandura
- Culture: United Arab Emirates
- Category: Dress
- Fact: The kandura, also known as the dishdasha, is an ankle-length, loose-fitting garment typically made of white cotton, worn by men in the United Arab Emirates with the ghutra headscarf.
- Source: Dubai government portal. https://www.dubai.ae/clothing

## 5. Bindi
- Culture: South Asia (Hindu and Jain traditions)
- Category: Adornment
- Fact: The word bindi comes from the Sanskrit bindu, meaning "drop" or "particle", and the mark is worn on the forehead between the eyebrows.
- Source: Encyclopaedia Britannica. https://www.britannica.com/topic/bindi
- Source: Hindu American Foundation. https://www.hinduamerican.org/blog/the-purpose-of-the-bindi/

## 6. Sugar skull (calavera)
- Culture: Mexico (Día de Muertos)
- Category: Ceremonial
- Fact: Sugar skulls are made for Día de Muertos, when families welcome the return of deceased relatives with offerings; UNESCO inscribed the festivity as Intangible Cultural Heritage in 2008.
- Source: UNESCO. https://ich.unesco.org/en/RL/indigenous-festivity-dedicated-to-the-dead-00054
- Source: National Geographic. https://www.nationalgeographic.com/history/article/dia-de-los-muertos

## 7. Raqs sharqi (belly dance)
- Culture: Egypt
- Category: Performance
- Fact: Raqs sharqi, Arabic for "eastern dance", took its staged form in Egypt's entertainment halls in the late nineteenth and early twentieth centuries.
- Source: AramcoWorld. https://www.aramcoworld.com/Resources/Suggestions/Egyptian-Belly-Dance-in-Transition-The-Raqs-Sharqi

## 8. Jerk chicken
- Culture: Jamaica (Caribbean)
- Category: Food
- Fact: Jerk developed in Jamaica from the fusion of African and Taíno cultures, and a dish counts as authentic jerk only when the meat is smoked over pimento wood.
- Source: Smithsonian Magazine. https://www.smithsonianmag.com/arts-culture/brief-history-jamaican-jerk-180976597/
